What is live, in build, or just shipped.

Every figure comes from a real engagement. Clients are described by sector and country. Named references are available in conversation.

  1. Facilities servicesItaly The back office runs on email. We turned it into a ledger. In production

    44,471emails and 63,556 attachments across four shared inboxes, six months

    A 20-person back office registers maintenance requests from banks and retail chains, dispatches technicians or subcontractors, chases signed intervention sheets, then invoices. Every job lives across many Outlook threads. We rebuilt the whole corpus into cases, one real-world job each, with every hand-off tracked, per-client behaviour profiles, and a dashboard the CEO now works from.

    • The unit of work is the case, not the email. Threads are stitched into one job. Each movement between parties is a hop with intent and attached documents.
    • Per-client profiles. The largest client's request formats, identifiers and closure documents are learned from history and approved by a person before they drive extraction.
    • Verification first. Ground truth was rebuilt from source documents before the pipeline was trusted with the full six-month corpus.

    Signals are detected, accounts enriched down to the VAT number, and pushed into the CRM with the fields the sales team actually uses. Deployed at three portfolio companies so far.

  4. Surface coatingsItaly From delivery notes to quotes to the production floor. Three workstreams

    5 to 15%area-estimate error from CAD files when drafting quotes, editable before anything is sent

    Started with one document: the delivery note. Phase two reads quotation emails and CAD drawings, reuses historical prices where they exist, otherwise estimates area and applies current pricing and commercial multipliers before drafting the reply. Phase three schedules the floor, carts, frames and treatment buffers, so profitability is visible by client, batch and day.

    • Phase one is always a review surface. The system proposes, shows its evidence, and a person approves in seconds. The human is never removed in v1.
    • Writeback path pinned on day one. Where the result lands in the client's system, and who provides that path, is settled before the first line of code.
    • Each phase funds the next. The delivery-note win bought the trust for quoting. Quoting bought the trust for scheduling.

Identify. Build. Run. Measure.

The same four moves at every altitude. What changes is who we sit next to.

  1. 1

    Days, on site

    Identify

    We walk the floor, sit in the inbox, read the ERP. The output is a written map of how the business really runs and a shortlist of bounded opportunities that survive fragmented systems.

    Two days at the lighting company produced the roadmap. One ERP backup at the diligence target replaced weeks of interviews.

  2. 2

    Weeks

    Build

    One workflow, on your data, in production. Structured pipelines rather than free-form generation. A review surface where a person approves. The writeback path into your system settled before we start.

    Delivery notes first at the coatings plant. The ticket dashboard at the facilities company was demoed to the CEO within weeks of the first data pull.

  3. 3

    Months

    Run

    We operate alongside the current process until the numbers match and the team trusts the output. Weekly feedback loops with the people using it. Bugs and enhancements tracked in the open.

    Weekly sessions with the head of sales shaped Signal phase two. Twice-weekly calls with the talent tool's owner through hypercare.

  4. 4

    Continuously

    Measure

    A shared framework across every engagement: a north-star outcome, then activation, adoption, performance and value-creation metrics per user journey, so the fund sees the same dashboard across its portfolio.

    Profitability by client, batch and day is the north star at the coatings plant. Sales-accepted leads is the one for Signal.

Ninety percent software. Ten percent AI.

The model is rarely the hard part. The data, the hand-offs, the exceptions, the writeback and the person who has to trust it. That is the work.

  • The human stays in v1

    Every first release is a review surface: the system proposes, shows its evidence, a person approves. Liability sits with the client. The design respects that.

  • Cost per unit is a design constraint

    If an LLM call per record costs more than the analyst it replaces, it is the wrong architecture. Classical methods do the bulk. Models do the residual.

  • Ground truth before pipelines

    We rebuild the truth from source documents by hand before we trust an extraction with a corpus. Where the system isn't certain, it says so.

  • Your data stays in your jurisdiction

    Registered in DIFC. EU environments for EU portfolio companies. Encrypted deliverables, deletion policies, and access we can account for.
