# AiStudio website brief

The storyline, content and sections for a simple, editorial site in the wonderful.ai register. No 3D. This document is self-contained: hand it to a new session and it has everything it needs.

---

## 1. The idea

**Go where the work is.**

Private equity buys companies at a distance from them. To a fund, a company is a row: revenue, margin, headcount, a hold period. The row is true and it explains almost nothing about where the time goes. The data room is what a company chooses to show. The work, and the value, sits further in: a business that runs on paper, email threads and a system from another decade that everyone has learned to work around.

AiStudio goes in there. Engineers sit inside the fund, inside the deal, and inside the companies themselves, and build one workflow at a time, in production, on the company's own data, until the paper becomes a system. What they learn on the floor changes how they read a data room. What they build for one company becomes a pattern for the next.

The site walks the reader **from the fund's seat to the desk where the work happens**, one step at a time, then pulls back to show that the same team works in all three places. Each step is closer to the work and more concrete than the last. The reader should feel the difference between AI sold as slides and AI built next to the people doing the job.

The earlier draft framed this as a descent through a building, with basement levels and an elevator. That language was tied to a 3D version that is no longer being built, and it read as a metaphor about architecture rather than about distance from the work. The chapters are now a plain numbered sequence.

One line for the whole site: *AiStudio builds AI for private equity the only way we know it works: with engineers inside the fund, inside the deal, and inside the companies themselves.*

---

## 2. Content rules

- Nothing traceable to a client. No figures, sectors, countries, timelines or engagement details. Describe the pattern, never the instance.
- Company facts are fine: founders, advisors, where the team has worked before. The DIFC registration is an address, not a claim: it belongs in the footer as one line, not in a sentence. Team size, office locations and where engineers deploy are not selling points and stay off the page.
- One conversion: **Book a conversation** → https://calendly.com/sharad-aistudio/30min. Fallback: sharad@aistudio.ae.
- Voice: quiet, precise, physical. Short sentences. Plain verbs. No hype words, no "leverage", no "unlock". Written by engineers who have shipped where mistakes cost money.
- No em-dashes in copy. No exclamation marks. Sentence case everywhere except the signage system (see §4).

## 3. Sections, in order

The page is one long scroll. Each section below gives the copy exactly as it should appear, then a note on what the section has to do.

### 3.1 Hero

- Body: AiStudio builds AI for private equity the only way we know it works: with engineers inside the fund, inside the deal, and inside the companies themselves.
- CTAs: **Book a conversation** (primary) · **How it works** (secondary, jumps to chapter 01)
- Hint: `Scroll`

Job: state the thesis in five words. Two CTAs side by side, as wonderful.ai does. No label above the headline, no stats, no logos.

### 3.2 The five chapters

One continuous sequence, numbered 01 to 05. A thin vertical rail on the right shows where the reader is and jumps to any chapter. The numeral sits in the left margin against a vertical rule, so the sequence reads as an index.

**01  The fund**
- Statement: **To a fund, a company is a row.**
- Body: Revenue, margin, headcount, a hold period. A partner sees forty of these. The row is true, and it explains almost nothing about where the time goes.

**02  The deal**
- Statement: **The data room is what a company chooses to show.**
- Body: Decks, contracts, audited accounts. We add one thing: read-only access to the systems the company actually runs on, and a working demo on its own data before the committee meets. Diligence you can click.

**03  The company**
- Statement: **Inside, the business runs on paper.**
- Body: Email threads twenty people long. Spreadsheets with six versions. A system from another decade that everyone has learned to work around. Nobody drew this. It grew. And it is where the value is.

**04  The floor**
- Statement: **This is where we sit.**
- Body: Our engineers work from the client's own desk: the back office, the plant, the fund's Monday meeting. We read the systems, sit with the people who do the work, and build with them. One workflow at a time, in production, on their data, in weeks.

**05  The sheet**
- Statement: **One document at a time, the paper becomes a system.**
- Body: A delivery note becomes a record. A quote request becomes a priced reply. A thread becomes a case with an owner. A person approves every result until the numbers match. Then we stay until it runs without us.

Job: this is the story. Five beats, each one step closer to the work and more concrete than the last. This is a real sequence, so numbering is earned here and nowhere else on the page.

No illustration per chapter. It was tried, with a small drawing for each, and it cheapened the page. The chapters carry themselves on type; the only figure on the site is the sheet, in chapter 05.

### 3.3 How we build

- Statement: **Ninety percent software. Ten percent AI.**
- Body: The model is the easy part. The hard part is the hand-off, the exception, the writeback, and the person who has to trust it. Four rules keep us honest.
- The four rules (two columns on desktop, one on mobile; no numbers, no icons):
  1. **The human stays in the first version.** The system proposes and shows its evidence. A person approves. Liability sits with the client, and the design respects that.
  2. **Cost per record is a design constraint.** If a model call costs more than the person it helps, it is the wrong architecture. Classical methods do the bulk. Models do the residual.
  3. **Ground truth before pipelines.** We rebuild the truth from source documents by hand before we trust a system with a corpus. Where it is not certain, it says so.
  4. **Your data stays in your jurisdiction.** EU environments for EU companies. Encrypted deliverables, deletion policies, and access we can account for.

Job: the value proposition, in the position wonderful.ai gives to "We bring together the platform, the deployment teams…". This is where a sceptical partner decides we are serious.

### 3.4 One team

- Statement: **The same team, in all three places.**
- Body: What we learn on the floor changes how we read a data room. What we build for one company becomes a pattern for the next. The fund sees the same measures across its portfolio, and the deal team sees what a company is really made of before it buys.
- Three short pillars (wonderful.ai's three-pillar move), each a bold lead and one sentence:
  - **Before the deal.** Diligence with working software as the output, not a slide on AI potential.
  - **Inside the fund.** Internal tools the fund's own team uses every week, built inside its secure environment.
  - **Inside the companies.** Production systems on the shop floor and in the back office, with engineers on site until it runs.

Job: pull back. After five chapters, name the three places the same team works. The rail calls this one "One team".

## 4. Design direction (wonderful.ai register, translated)

What wonderful.ai does that we want: a single column, text-forward, generous whitespace, no card grids, inline links instead of button arrays, punchy statements alternating with longer plain passages, two paired CTAs in the hero, a three-pillar section, a team section that reads as a mission, and a clean light palette.

What we add so it is ours and not a template:

- **The rail.** One thin vertical line fixed at the right edge, with a marker that moves as you scroll and the chapter names beside it. Together with the numeral index down the left of the text, this is the signature. Everything else stays quiet.
- **A living background.** The page sits on a slow mesh gradient with fine grain, from `@paper-design/shaders`, in a cool blue-grey family. It moves at about a twelfth of real time: it gives the page depth without asking to be looked at. The background does not change with scroll position.
- **Signage as the one small-caps system.** Chapter names, the rail labels and buttons use a condensed sans, uppercase, tracked. Nothing else on the page is uppercase. Never put a small tracked label above every heading; chapter names exist only in the five numbered chapters.
- **Palette.** One palette throughout, light. Off-white with a hint of blue, ink near-black, secondary text around 60 percent. One accent, a deep ochre, for interactive states only: hover fills, the rail marker, the active chapter's line, and the field highlights on the sheet. No gradients in the type, no glass, no gradient text.
- **Type.** One family for everything that speaks: Host Grotesk (variable, self-hosted in `public/fonts/`). Barlow Condensed for the signage. Statements at roughly clamp(2.2rem, 5vw, 4.8rem), weight 500, letter-spacing −0.02em, line-height 1.02, max 14 characters wide. Body 1.05rem, line-height 1.55, max 42 characters in the narrative sections and 65 elsewhere. Do not use Inter, DM Sans, Space Grotesk or any of the usual defaults.
- **Motion.** One page-load sequence (statement lines rise out of a mask, body fades in a beat later). Each chapter's name and statement reveal once as they cross the reading line; the rail marker follows scroll; the sheet is scrubbed. Hover on buttons fills from the left. Nothing bounces. Everything has a `prefers-reduced-motion` form that resolves instantly.
- **Imagery.** One figure only: the sheet. Do not add a decorative illustration per chapter; it was tried and it cheapened the page. No stock photography, no 3D, no icons.
- **Layout rhythm.** Hero at full viewport height. Chapters at about 84vh each so the reader dwells. How we build, Every altitude and Who we are as normal flow sections with large top margins. Start at 80vh with the CTA in the upper third.
- **Mobile.** Everything stacks, the numeral moving above the chapter name. The rail collapses to a marker-only line, then hides below 640px. Statements stay large; bodies stay short.

Register check before building: if someone could guess the layout from "AI consultancy landing page", change it. The descent and the rail are what make this ours.

## 5. Suggested build and skills

**Stack.** Static HTML and CSS with a small amount of JavaScript for the rail and reveals (GSAP with ScrollTrigger is already in this repo and is fine; plain IntersectionObserver also works). Astro or plain Vite are both reasonable. Self-host the two fonts from `public/fonts/`. Reuse the copy from `src/story.ts` if building inside this repo; the wording above is the current version of it.

**Skills to invoke, in order.**

1. `frontend-design` for the design plan: tokens, type scale, layout sketch, and the anti-template check before code. Feed it this brief.
2. `impeccable craft "AiStudio site from docs/site-brief.md"` to build. `PRODUCT.md` in this repo already holds the register, audience and anti-references; regenerate `DESIGN.md` for the light, 2D system with `impeccable document` once the first pass exists.
3. `impeccable typeset` on the statements and the descent, since type carries the whole page.
4. `impeccable animate` for the rail and the reveals, with reduced motion as a requirement.
5. `impeccable critique` then `impeccable polish` before shipping; `impeccable audit` for contrast, tab order and responsive behaviour; `impeccable adapt` if mobile needs a pass.
6. `superpowers:verification-before-completion` before claiming it is done: run the page at desktop and phone widths and check every anchor, the rail, both CTAs and reduced motion.

**Do not use** the 3D scene, the WebGL stack, or anything from `src/scene/`. The QA scripts in `scripts/` are for the 3D site and can be ignored.
